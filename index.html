Why this exists

“If you don't know you want to go to San Francisco, you might just end up in New York.” — from Zēteō

New York isn't a bad place. It just might not be where your heart was headed.

Most goal-setting starts too late. It hands you a template for a goal you've never actually examined — one you may have inherited from a parent, a peer group, or a version of yourself from ten years ago. You get very efficient at climbing a ladder leaning against the wrong wall.

The outcome frame starts one step earlier. Before how, it asks what — and then keeps asking, from enough different angles that a goal you only half-believed in can't survive the questioning.

What's left at the end is something you can actually run toward.

The Method

The outcome frame, in four movements

The sequence matters. Each movement does work the next one depends on — which is why Skip asks them one at a time, and won't let you skate past an answer that isn't yet true. Read the full reference →

Movement One

Destination

What you want, stated in the positive — and the value underneath it that makes it worth wanting.

  1. What specifically do you want?
  2. What will having it do for you?
Movement Two

Reality

An honest starting point, and the resistance that lives inside you rather than out in the world.

  1. Where are you now?
  2. What stops you from having it?
Movement Three

Design

Context, evidence, and ecology — shaping a goal specific enough to recognize and safe enough to keep.

  1. When, where, how, with whom?
  2. Does it fit other situations?
  3. How would you know you have it?
  4. What will you see, hear, feel?
  5. How do you appear to others?
  6. Is this outcome only for you?
  7. Is it OK for you to have it?
  8. Is the outcome worthwhile?
  9. Does it fit your life as a whole?
  10. How will it affect the people close to you?
Movement Four

Becoming

Identity, honest accounting of the cost, and one step small enough to take today.

  1. Is it representative of who you want to be?
  2. What do you stand to gain?
  3. Will you lose anything of value?
  4. What is your first step?

Your guide

Meet Skip

Skip is not a form. Skip is an interviewer — one that listens for the difference between what you said and what you meant, and asks again when they don't match.

S
SkipLet's start at the beginning. What specifically do you want?
YOU
YouI want to stop feeling stuck in my career.
S
SkipThat's a real thing to feel, and I hear it. Here's what I notice: you've told me what you want to get away from. Your mind can't move toward the absence of something.

So — if "stuck" were behind you, what would you be moving toward instead?
  • It reframes without lecturing youNegative goals get turned toward something. Vague goals get made specific. You'll never hear the word "reframe" — just a better question.
  • It works with the part of you that's hesitantWhen one part wants the goal and another part is nervous, Skip explores what the cautious part is protecting rather than talking you out of it.
  • It won't let a goal pass that isn't yoursIf your outcome depends on someone else's approval, Skip helps you rebuild it into something you can start and sustain on your own.
  • It ends with something you can holdA two-paragraph summary — what you want, why you want it, and your first step — plus the full transcript of everything you worked through.

Two ways to walk it

By yourself, or with someone beside you

Some questions land harder when there's another person in the room. GoalX works either way — you choose at the start.

Solo

Just you and Skip

You type, Skip responds. Go at your own pace, leave and come back inside the same session, and take as long as any question deserves.

Best for people who think by writing, and for anyone who'd be less honest with an audience.

Coach-led

A guide at the keyboard

A coach opens the session, reads each question aloud, and types the client's answers as they speak. Skip handles the sequence and the reframes; the coach handles the human being.

Coach mode adds a private prompt panel — what to listen for on each question, and what to ask if the answer stays on the surface.

Your answers

Nothing is stored. Anywhere.

These questions only work if you tell the truth, and people don't tell the truth to a database. So there isn't one.

No account, no sign-in

You don't create anything. You open the page and start. There's nothing to log into and nothing to delete later.

It lives in your browser

Your answers are held in the page's memory while you work. Close the tab and they're gone — not archived, not backed up, not recoverable.

You choose how it leaves

At the end, download your summary and transcript as a document, or email it to yourself. Either way it's a one-time hand-off — no copy is kept.
